# Scout research playbook

Read this at the start of every scout run. The scout's job is not to find one article; it is to understand what is working right now for this topic and format, and to hand the writer and the designer enough evidence and visual references that they never start from a blank page.

## Depth

A full run meets all of these minimums. When a minimum cannot be met, say which and why in the scout's report.

- At least 12 dated sources, from at least 4 of the source types below.
- At least 3 rounds of searching: broad, then narrowed by what the first round showed, then a round aimed at the gaps.
- At least 8 visual references and at least 3 motion references for any video format.
- Every claim the writer might use has a source no older than 18 months, unless it is a stable fact, which you mark as such.

## Source types

| Type | Where to look | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| Platform signals | the platform's own creator blog, help pages, and announcements | formats and lengths it promotes, new features |
| Creator examples | public posts, reels, and videos on the topic found through web search | hooks, structure, pacing, what the comments react to |
| Industry analysis | marketing and social media publications, research reports with a date | measured patterns, benchmarks with their sample |
| Visual inspiration | Pinterest, Behance, Dribbble, design galleries | layout, palette moods, typography treatments |
| Motion inspiration | motion design showcases, title sequence archives, public reels | transitions, kinetic type, pacing |
| Topic facts | primary sources: official sites, papers, documentation, reputable news | the facts the piece states |

## Searching visual sites

- Pinterest pages often need a login. Search with the site name in the query (for example `pinterest editorial carousel typography`) and read what public pin pages and image results show. Never try to log in or bypass a wall.
- On any gallery, follow the original creator's page when it is linked and cite that, not the aggregator.
- Use any stock or image search connector you have to find licensed images that could become assets; record their licence and the photographer.

## Saving references

- Save reference images to `scout-refs/` in the run folder as `ref-01.jpg` and onward, at most 30 per run and at most 2 MB each.
- Every saved file gets a row in `scout.md` under `## Visual references` or `## Motion references`: the file or link, the source URL, the creator when known, the date read, the class, and what to take from it (for example "staggered word reveal on the hook", "two-colour duotone over a blurred photo").
- The class is `reference-only` for anything found on the open web. Only an image from a licensed stock source may be marked `candidate-asset`, with its licence; the main session decides whether it enters `assets.md` under the media policy.
- For video references, save a link and, when you can, 3 to 6 still frames that show the move; describe the motion in words, because a still cannot show it.
- Never save images showing private messages, personal documents, or faces of private individuals.

## Turning evidence into direction

After research, write for each of these one short paragraph with the sources that support it:

1. **Angle:** the single idea the piece commits to.
2. **Hooks:** at least 3 options, each with the pattern it follows and an example that used it.
3. **Format and structure:** the length, page or scene count, and order that the evidence favours.
4. **Visual direction:** the mood, palette use within the brand's roles, layout patterns, and type treatment, each tied to references.
5. **Motion direction:** for video, the pacing, the transition styles, and the kinetic type patterns, each tied to motion references.
6. **Avoid:** what is overused or saturated right now on this topic, with examples.
